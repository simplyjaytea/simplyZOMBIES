# A pole run is poles in one street lane, and each pole draws a run after it

- **Date:** 2026-11-12
- **Decided by:** engineering
- **Systems:** street dressing (`godot/presentation/first_person/street_dressing.gd`)

**Decision.** Two poles are in a run when their tile rows (or columns) differ by at most
`POLE_LANE_TILES` (6, so both kerbs of one street are one lane), they stand 10 to 40 m apart
centre to centre, and no Wall tile lies on the line between them. Each pole is wired to its
nearest partner on each side of each axis, three wires a pair. Placement grows a run from each
seed pole (the best-ranked clear candidate on each side, spacing still 25 m) before the next
seed is taken. A corner pole carries a link on each axis and is turned across its shortest link.
A pole with no partner stays and carries no wire.

**Why.** After #394, 28 of 34 poles were bare: the old rule joined only poles on one exact tile
row, and the hash scatter put almost none there. Measured at seed 31337 on the four districts
at 128 tiles, 5 to 10 of 12 to 16 poles are wired now, and none with a partner is bare.
Wire-over-wall sampling is every half metre because nine samples missed thin walls.

**Instead of.**

- Diagonal spans between any two poles: they cross lots, and the crossarm cannot face them.
- Dropping lone poles: it lowers the pole density the owner set.
- Raising the pole weight so every pole has a partner: a visible density change, the owner's.
